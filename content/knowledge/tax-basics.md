# Tax Basics

## Disclaimer

This is general education, not tax advice. Consult a CPA or tax professional for your specific situation. Tax laws change frequently.

## Key Concepts

### Marginal Tax Rates
You don't pay one rate on all income. Each bracket only applies to income within that range.

**Example (2025 Single):**
- First $11,925: 10%
- $11,926 - $48,475: 12%
- $48,476 - $103,350: 22%
- $103,351 - $197,300: 24%
- And so on...

**Common misconception:** "Getting a raise pushed me into a higher bracket so I take home less." **Wrong.** Only the income above the bracket threshold is taxed at the higher rate.

### Standard vs. Itemized Deductions

| Filing Status | 2025 Standard Deduction |
|--------------|------------------------|
| Single | $15,000 |
| Married Filing Jointly | $30,000 |
| Head of Household | $22,500 |

**Itemize when** your deductions exceed the standard deduction. Common itemized deductions:
- Mortgage interest
- State and local taxes (SALT, capped at $10,000)
- Charitable contributions
- Medical expenses exceeding 7.5% of AGI

**Most people:** Standard deduction wins. Don't overthink it.

### Tax Credits vs. Deductions

| | Deduction | Credit |
|---|-----------|--------|
| What it does | Reduces taxable income | Reduces tax owed directly |
| Example | $1,000 deduction in 22% bracket saves $220 | $1,000 credit saves $1,000 |
| Value | Depends on your bracket | Same value regardless of bracket |

**Credits are more valuable.** Always claim every credit you qualify for.

## Common Credits Worth Knowing

- **Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC)** — Low to moderate income, can be thousands
- **Child Tax Credit** — Up to $2,000 per qualifying child
- **Saver's Credit** — Contributing to retirement accounts on low/moderate income
- **Education Credits** — American Opportunity (up to $2,500) and Lifetime Learning
- **EV Tax Credit** — For qualifying electric vehicles

## Tax-Advantaged Strategies

### Reduce Taxable Income
- Max out 401(k) contributions (pre-tax)
- Contribute to Traditional IRA (if deductible)
- HSA contributions (if eligible) — triple tax advantage
- Flexible Spending Account (FSA) for healthcare/dependent care

### Time Income and Deductions
- If expecting lower income next year, defer income if possible
- If expecting higher income, accelerate deductions into current year
- Bunch charitable donations into one year to exceed standard deduction

### Capital Gains
- **Short-term** (<1 year): Taxed as ordinary income
- **Long-term** (>1 year): Taxed at 0%, 15%, or 20% depending on income
- **Lesson:** Hold investments for at least a year when possible

## Annual Tax Calendar

| When | What |
|------|------|
| January | Receive W-2s, 1099s. Start gathering documents. |
| January 15 | Q4 estimated tax payment due (if applicable) |
| April 15 | Tax filing deadline (or file extension) |
| April 15 | Q1 estimated tax payment due |
| June 15 | Q2 estimated tax payment due |
| September 15 | Q3 estimated tax payment due |
| October 15 | Extended filing deadline |
| Year-end | Last chance for charitable donations, IRA contributions to existing accounts, tax-loss harvesting |

## When to Get Professional Help

- Self-employment income
- Rental property income
- Stock options or RSUs
- Major life events (marriage, divorce, inheritance)
- Business ownership
- Multi-state income
- Complex investment situations
- IRS correspondence or audit
