# Debt Payoff Strategies

## Avalanche Method (Math-Optimal)

Pay minimums on everything, throw all extra money at the **highest interest rate** debt first.

**How it works:**
1. List all debts by interest rate (highest first)
2. Pay minimums on all debts
3. Put every extra dollar toward the highest-rate debt
4. When it's paid off, roll that payment to the next highest
5. Repeat until debt-free

**Pros:** Saves the most money in total interest paid
**Cons:** If your highest-rate debt is also your largest, progress feels slow

**Best for:** Analytical people motivated by math and total savings.

## Snowball Method (Motivation-Optimal)

Pay minimums on everything, throw all extra money at the **smallest balance** first.

**How it works:**
1. List all debts by balance (smallest first)
2. Pay minimums on all debts
3. Put every extra dollar toward the smallest balance
4. When it's paid off, roll that payment to the next smallest
5. Repeat — each payoff builds momentum

**Pros:** Quick wins build motivation. Fewer bills to manage faster.
**Cons:** May pay more total interest than avalanche

**Best for:** People who need motivation wins, those with many small debts.

## Hybrid Approach

Combine both methods:
- Attack high-rate debt first (>15%)
- For similar rates, target the smallest balance
- The interest rate difference between 5% and 7% is minimal — go for the motivational win

## Debt Consolidation

Combine multiple debts into a single payment, ideally at a lower rate.

### Options
| Method | Best For | Watch Out For |
|--------|----------|---------------|
| Balance transfer card (0% intro) | Credit card debt <$10k | Transfer fees (3-5%), rate after promo |
| Personal loan | Multiple high-rate debts | Origination fees, must qualify |
| Home equity (HELOC) | Large debts, homeowners | Your home is collateral |
| Student loan refinance | Private student loans | Lose federal protections |

**Key rule:** Consolidation only helps if you stop accumulating new debt. It's a tool, not a cure.

## Refinancing

Replace existing debt with a new loan at better terms.

**When to refinance:**
- You can get 1%+ lower interest rate
- You've improved your credit score significantly
- Rates have dropped market-wide
- You want to change the loan term

**When NOT to refinance:**
- You're close to paying off the original loan
- Fees exceed the interest savings
- You'd extend the term and pay more total
- Federal student loans with income-driven plans (refinancing makes them private)

## Emergency Debt Protocol

If you're in crisis mode (can't make minimums):

1. **Triage:** Pay essentials first (housing, food, utilities, transportation)
2. **Contact creditors** — hardship programs exist. Call before you miss payments.
3. **Minimum payments** on everything else — protect your credit score
4. **Cut aggressively** — cancel subscriptions, reduce discretionary to near-zero temporarily
5. **Increase income** — overtime, side gig, sell unused items
6. **Seek help** — nonprofit credit counseling (NFCC) is free and legitimate

**Avoid:** Payday loans, high-fee debt settlement companies, ignoring the problem (it gets worse).

## Debt-to-Income Ratio

| DTI Range | Status | Action |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| Under 20% | Healthy | Stay the course, focus on building wealth |
| 20-35% | Manageable | Prioritize payoff, avoid new debt |
| 36-49% | Stressed | Aggressive payoff plan, no new debt |
| 50%+ | Crisis | Emergency protocol, consider professional help |

**Calculate:** (Total monthly debt payments ÷ Gross monthly income) × 100
