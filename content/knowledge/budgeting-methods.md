# Budgeting Methods

## 50/30/20 Rule

The simplest starting framework. Divide after-tax income into three buckets:

| Category | % of Income | What's Included |
|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Needs** | 50% | Rent/mortgage, utilities, groceries, insurance, minimum debt payments, transportation |
| **Wants** | 30% | Dining out, entertainment, subscriptions, shopping, hobbies |
| **Savings/Debt** | 20% | Emergency fund, extra debt payments, retirement, investments |

**Best for:** Beginners, people who want simple guardrails without tracking every dollar.

**Adjust when:** Housing costs exceed 30% of income (common in HCOL areas) — compress wants to compensate.

## Zero-Based Budget

Every dollar gets a job. Income minus all planned expenses equals exactly zero.

**Process:**
1. Start with total monthly income
2. List every expense category with planned amounts
3. Assign remaining dollars to savings/debt goals
4. Track actual vs. planned throughout the month
5. Adjust categories monthly based on reality

**Best for:** People who want maximum control, detail-oriented planners, those with variable income.

**Watch out for:** Burnout from over-tracking. Build in a "miscellaneous" category for flexibility.

## Envelope Method

Cash-based (or virtual envelope) system where each spending category gets a fixed amount.

**Process:**
1. Define spending categories (groceries, gas, dining, entertainment, etc.)
2. Set a monthly amount for each
3. When an envelope is empty, stop spending in that category
4. Leftover rolls to next month or goes to savings

**Best for:** People who overspend with cards, those who need physical/visual spending limits.

**Modern adaptation:** Use separate bank accounts or budgeting app "buckets" instead of literal cash envelopes.

## Pay Yourself First

Automate savings/investments immediately when income arrives. Spend what's left.

**Process:**
1. Set up automatic transfers on payday
2. Savings, retirement, debt payments go first
3. Bills and necessities next (also automated)
4. What remains is spending money

**Best for:** People who save what's "left over" (hint: there's never anything left over). Great combined with any other method.

## Anti-Budget (80/20)

Save 20% automatically. Spend the other 80% however you want. No tracking required.

**Process:**
1. Automate 20% of income to savings/investments
2. Pay fixed bills
3. Spend the rest guilt-free
4. Review quarterly to see if 20% should increase

**Best for:** People who hate budgeting but still want to build wealth. High earners with good spending instincts.

## Choosing a Method

| If You... | Try This |
|-----------|----------|
| Want simplicity | 50/30/20 or Anti-Budget |
| Want maximum control | Zero-Based |
| Overspend on cards | Envelope Method |
| Always forget to save | Pay Yourself First |
| Have irregular income | Zero-Based (use lowest expected month as baseline) |
| Are in a debt crisis | Zero-Based + aggressive debt allocation |

**Key insight:** The best budget is the one you'll actually follow. Start simple, add complexity only if it helps.
